# Production spec: cinematic slow tour

Read `../INSTA-PRODUCTION-NOTES.md` first, with one override: **shoot and deliver this format
at 24fps**, not 30. It is the only format in the set where the frame rate is part of the
look.

**Target length:** 18 to 25 seconds
**Crew:** one operator with a gimbal, one person to clear rooms ahead of the camera
**Site time:** 2 hours
**Post time:** 2 hours, most of it grading

This format wins on **saves**, not shares or comments. Judge it on that and nothing else.
A slow tour with 400 views and 60 saves is doing its job. A slow tour with 40,000 views
and 12 saves has failed, whatever it looks like on the dashboard.

---

## Part A: the site has to be ready

More than any other format here, this one is decided before the camera comes out. A
cinematic tour of a badly prepared flat is a cinematic tour of a badly prepared flat.

**Two hours before the shoot:**
- Every surface clear. No bottles, no cables, no site notices, no shoe racks.
- All lights on, including the ones you would not normally switch on in daytime. Warm
  bulbs, not cool white. Cool white LED is what makes Indian sample flats look like
  clinics on camera.
- Curtains open, windows clean. Window glass with cement film on it destroys every
  interior shot and cannot be fixed in post.
- Fans off. A moving fan at 24fps with a 1/48 shutter strobes badly.
- One styled object per room, no more. A single vase, a folded throw, a book. Three
  styled objects read as a furniture catalogue.

If the flat is not ready, reschedule. This is the one format where shooting anyway
produces nothing usable.

---

## Part B: camera settings

- 24fps, shutter 1/48 or 1/50. This is what gives the motion its weight. At 1/250 the
  movement looks digital and the format collapses.
- On a phone, that means using a pro video mode with manual shutter, or an ND filter in
  daylight. Without shutter control you will not get this look, and you should shoot
  format 17 instead.
- Widest aperture your lens allows, for separation.
- Fixed white balance per room. Auto white balance shifting mid-move is the most common
  amateur tell in interior video.
- Flat or log profile if available, because the grade is doing half the work here.

---

## Part C: the shot list, in order

Seven to nine shots, each 3.0 to 4.0 seconds, each a single uninterrupted move. No shot
contains a change of direction.

| # | Shot | Move | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The approach** | Slow push toward the front door or the building entrance | Establishes arrival. Low, 100 cm. |
| 2 | **The threshold** | Push through the doorway into the living room | The move continues through the frame line. This is the shot that makes it feel like a tour and not a slideshow. |
| 3 | **The living reveal** | Slow lateral slide left to right across the living space | Let a foreground element pass close to the lens. Depth is what sells a small room. |
| 4 | **The light** | Static or a 2 percent push on the window and what the light falls on | Every strong version of this format has one shot that is about light rather than a room. |
| 5 | **The kitchen** | Push along the counter line, ending on the far wall | Low, counter height. |
| 6 | **The detail** | Static, close: a handle, a tap, a tile edge, a switch plate | 3 seconds. This is the save-generating shot. People save interiors for details, not for wide angles. |
| 7 | **The bedroom** | Push from the door toward the window | |
| 8 | **The balcony or the view** | Push out through the balcony door, ending on the outside | Reverse of shot 2. The tour leaves the way it came in. |
| 9 | **The hold** | Static wide, exterior or living room, 3 seconds | Where the single line of copy sits. |

**Rules that apply to every shot:**
- Start recording 2 seconds before the move and stop 2 seconds after. Handles give the
  editor somewhere to cut.
- Move slower than feels right. Whatever pace you think is slow, halve it.
- No whip pans, no speed ramps, no snap zooms. Those belong to format 17 and they undo
  everything this format is buying.
- Shoot each one three times. The third take is where the operator's walking rhythm
  settles.

---

## Part D: the edit

- Cut on the move, not between moves. The outgoing shot should still be moving when the
  incoming shot starts, and in roughly the same direction. This is what makes eight
  separate clips feel like one continuous walk.
- Cross dissolves of 8 to 12 frames between shots 3 and 4 and between 7 and 8. Hard cuts
  elsewhere. A fully dissolved edit gets soupy.
- No speed changes anywhere.
- **Grade:** lift the shadows slightly so they go soft rather than black, pull the
  highlights down, push warmth into the highlights and let the shadows stay neutral or
  slightly cool. Desaturate greens, which in Indian interiors usually come from a
  fluorescent tube or a balcony plant and read as sickly on camera.
- Do not use a LUT pack unmodified. Every one of them is calibrated for a different
  source and they all push contrast too hard for interiors.

**Audio:** one piece of music, no ambient, no voiceover. Something with a slow build and
no percussion hits. The music should end at the last frame, not fade out, because a fade
on a reel that loops sounds like a mistake.

---

## Part E: text on screen

One text event. That is the discipline of this format and it is the whole point.

- Appears at the last shot, around 18s, upper third, 44pt, thin weight, generous
  letter spacing.
- One line. Options:
  `Ashwin Residences. Kalyan West.`
  `Three sides open. Every floor.`
  `Possession December 2027.`
- The lockup follows at 21s, and for this format use a **reduced** lockup: project name
  and RERA number only, no price, no QR. If you must show the price, this is the wrong
  format for that listing.

The reason to hold back the price here is not aesthetic. This format is for premium
inventory where the buyer is deciding on feel and then asking the price. Leading with the
number moves the conversation to comparison, which is exactly where you do not want a
premium product to compete.

---

## Part F: caption

The caption carries everything the reel deliberately left out.

```
Ashwin Residences, Kalyan West.

3 BHK | 1,180 sq ft carpet | three sides open on every floor
Possession December 2027

MahaRERA P52100012345 | maharera.maharashtra.gov.in

Site visits on weekends. DM VISIT.

#kalyanwest #3bhk #interiordesign #mumbairealestate #architecture
```

Note the hashtags: `#interiordesign` and `#architecture` belong on this format and on no
other one in this library. They reach a design-interested audience that saves rather than
comments, which is the behaviour this format is built for.

---

## Part G: where this fits

Run this once per project, not per month, and only for inventory where it is warranted.
A cinematic tour of a Rs 45 L flat is a mismatch and viewers feel it.

Its real use is not Instagram reach at all. It is the file you send to a serious enquiry,
put on the project landing page, and play on a screen at the site office. Instagram is
where it lives publicly, but the 60 people who save it are worth more than the 4,000 who
scroll past.
