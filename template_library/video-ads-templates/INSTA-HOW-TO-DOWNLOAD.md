# How to download the reference reels

Every reel in folders 13 to 19 is linked in three places:

| File | What the links are |
|---|---|
| `README.md` (in each folder) | The reference reels, with the account, engagement numbers, and a note on why that one was picked |
| `DISCOVERED.md` (in each folder) | Further reels in the same format, found beyond your original 13 |
| `DOWNLOAD-LIST.txt` (in each folder) | Every URL for that folder, each paired with the exact filename to save it under |
| `INSTA-DOWNLOAD-LIST-ALL.csv` (here) | All 73 in one sheet, if you would rather work from a spreadsheet |

73 URLs total: 13 originals you supplied, 60 discovered.

**43 of them are already downloaded and sitting in their folders.** The lists remain as
the full catalogue of what was found, so you can pull down more later without rediscovering
them. `REELS-ON-DISK.md` in each folder tells you which ones you actually have.

---

## First, the part that matters more than the method

These are other people's videos. Downloading them as **reference for study** is normal
practice and is what this library is for. Reposting one to your own account, or cutting
it into your own reel, is a different thing: it is a copyright issue and it breaches
Instagram's terms, and on a business account it is the kind of thing that gets a strike.

Use them to study the cut, the timing, the text placement, the CTA. Then shoot your own
version using the `PRODUCTION-SPEC.md` in that folder. That is the whole point of the set.

---

## Method A: no download at all

On your phone, open each link and tap the bookmark icon, then **Save to Collection**.
Make one collection per format and name it to match the folder.

Needs no tools, and keeps the reels playable with their audio and their comment section,
which is often where the useful information is. The downside is they live in your
Instagram account rather than on your drive, and a creator deleting a post takes it with
them.

---

## Method B: actual files on the drive

You will need a downloader. Any paste-a-link service works the same way, and they come
and go, so if one is down try another.

The loop, per reel:

1. Open the folder's `DOWNLOAD-LIST.txt` and copy the first URL.
2. Paste it into the downloader and download the file.
3. Rename the downloaded file to the `save as:` name printed directly under that URL.
4. Move it into that folder.
5. Next URL.

**Work top to bottom, one folder at a time.** The list is already in the order you want,
originals first and discovered second, so if you go in order the numbering comes out
right without you having to think about it.

**A faster variant:** download a whole folder's worth into your Downloads folder first,
without renaming anything, then rename them all in one pass at the end. Windows Explorer
sorts by download time, and the list is in the same order, so they line up row for row.

### If a downloader refuses a link
Some of these are on accounts that restrict embedding. Two fallbacks:
- Open the reel on desktop, full screen, and screen record it. Quality is lower but for
  a reference clip that does not matter.
- Skip it. Every folder has more references than you need, and `README.md` already
  describes what that reel does.

## Naming and why it is strict

Files follow the same convention as the rest of the library:

```
reel__<folder>__<NN>-<creator-or-description>.mp4

reel__13-ai-land-to-home-transformation__02-plot2homeindia.mp4
```

The `save as:` line in each `DOWNLOAD-LIST.txt` gives you the exact string. Copy it
rather than typing it.

**The numbers are not contiguous, and that is deliberate.** Folder 13 holds 01, 02, 04,
10, 11, 15, 16 because the others were reviewed and discarded. The numbering is kept
tied to the download list so a file on disk still traces back to its source URL and to
the note explaining why it was picked. Renumbering them 01 to 07 would break that.

---

## You do not need the files

The `PRODUCTION-SPEC.md` in each folder is written to stand alone. It has the shot list,
the prep, the beat timing, the text and lockup placement, and the caption. None of it
depends on having the reference video open.
