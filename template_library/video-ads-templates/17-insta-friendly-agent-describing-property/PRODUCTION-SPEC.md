# Production spec: agent describing the property to camera

Read `../INSTA-PRODUCTION-NOTES.md` first.

**Target length:** 45 to 70 seconds
**Crew:** presenter, one operator, lav mic
**Site time:** 90 minutes
**Post time:** 3 hours, and most of that is the text

This is the longest format in the set and the only one that can carry actual information.
It is also the only one where post-production takes longer than the shoot, because the
kinetic text has to be cut to the voice word by word.

---

## Part A: the text is the format

Everything else here is in service of this one thing, so understand it before you plan a
shoot.

The reel has to work with the sound off. On a muted scroll the viewer sees a person
talking, which is nothing, plus text arriving in rhythm, which is everything. Both
presenter references run text through effectively every second of runtime. There is no
quiet stretch where the viewer is expected to just listen.

That produces three rules:

1. **Write the script and the supers as one document.** Not a script that you later add
   text to. The same sentence, broken into the chunks that will land on screen.
2. **Three to five words per card, never a full sentence.** The eye reads a card in about
   400ms. A ten-word line means the viewer is still reading while the presenter has moved
   on, and they drop.
3. **Set it enormous.** The text in both references occupies a third to a half of the
   frame width. Timid 32pt captions across the bottom are a different format and a worse
   one.

---

## Part B: writing it

Structure for a 60 second reel:

| Beat | Time | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0.0 to 2.0s | A claim or a challenge aimed at the viewer. Not a description. |
| Set-up | 2.0 to 8.0s | Name what this is and where. Locality plus one landmark. |
| The list | 8.0 to 35.0s | Four or five specifics, one per beat, each with its own text card. |
| The objection | 35.0 to 47.0s | Name the worry and answer it. |
| Price | 47.0 to 54.0s | Spoken and supered at the same moment. |
| CTA | 54.0 to 60.0s | One action. |

**On the hook.** The two presenter references both open on something aimed at the viewer
rather than at the property. One frames it as a challenge, the other as a negative claim
that sets up a reveal. Either works. What does not work is opening with the property's
name, because nobody scrolling has a reason to care yet.

Keep your hook honest. A challenge framing that the reel then fails to pay off costs you
more than a flat opening would have.

**On the list.** This is where the format earns its length. Five specifics, each a card:

```
620 sq ft carpet
Corner flat, three sides open
Covered parking included
8 minutes to the station
Possession December 2027
```

Each of those is a beat. The presenter says it, the card lands on the stressed word, the
card clears before the next one starts.

---

## Part C: the shoot

### Coverage
A minute on one angle is unwatchable. Shoot four setups minimum:

1. **Presenter wide.** Standing at the entrance or approach, property filling the
   background on both sides of them. Your opener and your CTA.
2. **Presenter mid.** Chest up, tighter, different position. Cut here every time the
   subject changes.
3. **Presenter walking.** Moving toward or alongside camera, 20 seconds of usable
   movement. Break up the static setups.
4. **Property b-roll.** Eight to ten clips at 3 seconds. The specific things the list
   mentions, one clip per claim. If you say "three sides open", show it.

Add lifestyle b-roll if it fits the inventory. The lakehouse reference cuts to a car,
which is doing work: it is selling a life, not a floor plan. For an Indian mid-market
project the equivalent is the station walk, the school gate, the morning market.

### Settings
- 4K if you have it, so you can punch in and create a second angle from one setup.
- Lav mic, non-negotiable. The text is cut to the voice.
- Shoot the presenter setups in the golden hour if the exterior matters, mid-morning
  otherwise.
- Lock exposure per setup.

### Delivery
The presenter is not performing to a sound here, they are explaining something. Slower
and clearer than a trending-audio reel. Have them say each list item as a complete
thought with a small pause after it, because those pauses are where the text cards land.

---

## Part D: the edit

1. **Cut the voice first.** Build the full audio track with the presenter's takes,
   trimmed tight, before you place a single text card or b-roll clip.
2. **Transcribe and chunk.** Break the transcript into three-to-five word cards. Most
   editors will auto-caption and give you a starting point, but the automatic chunking
   is always wrong and always needs rebreaking by hand.
3. **Place cards on the stressed word**, not on the first word of the phrase. This is the
   single thing that separates a reel that feels produced from one that feels templated.
4. **Animate simply.** Scale from 92 to 100 percent over 4 frames, or a fast fade. The
   references shift colour per word within a card, which is cheap and effective. No
   spinning, no typewriter, no bouncing.
5. **Cut picture last.** With voice and text locked, place the b-roll so each claim's
   clip lands under its card. Change angle at least every 5 seconds.
6. **Captions as well as cards.** Small burned-in captions at the bottom for everything
   the big cards do not cover. Accessibility, and it catches the viewer who is reading
   rather than listening.

---

## Part E: the lockup

Standard price and MahaRERA lockup from the shared notes, at the price beat (around 47s),
holding through the CTA.

Because this format runs long and states specifics, it carries more compliance weight
than the short ones. Everything you say on camera is a representation. If you say
"possession December 2027", that must be the RERA date, not your internal target.

## Part F: caption

```
620 sq ft in Kalyan West, and why the corner flats go first.

Covered parking | 8 min to Kalyan station
From Rs 78.5 L*
RERA possession: December 2027

MahaRERA P52100012345 | maharera.maharashtra.gov.in
*Price as on date of posting and subject to revision.

DM CORNER for the floor plan.

#kalyanwest #2bhkflat #thaneproperty #mumbairealestate
```

The second `_kalsie` reference puts the entire spec sheet in the caption and keeps the
video light. That split is worth copying: the video persuades, the caption informs.

## Part G: how this differs from 16

They look similar and they are not the same tool.

| | 16 presenter site price reveal | 17 agent describing the property |
|---|---|---|
| Where | Live construction site, PPE | Finished or sample property |
| Length | 22 to 28s | 45 to 70s |
| Selling | Credibility and progress | The product itself |
| Carries | One number | Five specifics and an objection |
| Inventory | Under construction | Ready or sample flat |

Run 16 while the slab is going up. Run 17 once there is something finished to stand in
front of. Running both on the same project across its life is the intended pattern.
