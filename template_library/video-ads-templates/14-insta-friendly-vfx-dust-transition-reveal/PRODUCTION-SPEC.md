# Production spec: VFX dust / smoke transition reveal

Read `../INSTA-PRODUCTION-NOTES.md` first.

**Target length:** 7 to 9 seconds
**Crew:** one operator, one person to throw the dust
**Site time:** 30 minutes
**Post time:** 45 minutes once the dust element is in your library

This is the budget version of format 13 and in many cases the better choice. It needs no
AI generation and no matched render, because the dust hides the seam. If you have an
existing building or a completed sample flat, this is the fastest strong reel you can
make.

---

## Part A: what the dust is actually doing

The dust is a cut hider. It gives you permission for the two shots to not match. That is
the whole mechanic, and understanding it changes what you shoot.

Because of that, this format works for transitions that format 13 cannot do:
- Bare plot to finished building (different sites, even)
- Old building to new elevation
- Empty shell interior to furnished interior
- Day to night
- Exterior to interior, in one move

Format 13 requires a matched camera. This one does not. That is its advantage, not a
compromise.

---

## Part B: getting the dust element

Three ways, in order of how good they look.

**B1. Shoot it real (best).**
- Dry soil, sand, or lime powder. Site dust is ideal because it matches the location.
- Camera at 120fps or 240fps if your phone supports it. Shooting at normal frame rate
  gives you a dust puff that is over in four frames and unusable.
- Person stands just outside frame and throws a double handful **toward the camera**, at
  a slight upward angle. Not across the frame. Toward it. You want the dust to fill the
  lens, not to pass by.
- Shoot against the actual scene, not against a wall. Backlight it if you can: dust
  against a bright sky reads far better than dust against shade.
- 6 to 8 throws. You need one where the dust fully occludes the frame.

**B2. Shoot it clean and composite.**
- Same throw, but against a dark background in shade, so you can screen-blend it over
  anything later.
- More flexible, slightly more obviously a VFX element.

**B3. Stock.**
- Free dust and sand transition packs are widely available for After Effects and
  CapCut. Fine as a starting point, but every second real estate account uses the same
  three packs and viewers notice.

Shoot your own once and you have it forever. It takes ten minutes.

---

## Part C: the shot list

### Plate A: the before
- Locked off, tripod, 10 seconds. Bare plot, old building, or empty room.
- Whatever it is, hold it long enough that the viewer registers what they are looking at.
  1.5 seconds on screen minimum.

### Plate B: the after
- The finished building, sample flat, or furnished room.
- **Camera does not need to match Plate A**, but the two should share a general
  direction of view. A hard reversal of angle across the dust reads as a mistake rather
  than a reveal.
- Add a slow push in, 3 to 4 percent across the shot. Motion after the dust clears is
  what makes the reveal feel like an arrival.

### Plate C: the dust
Per Part B.

## Part D: the edit, beat by beat

30fps, 8 seconds.

| Time | Frames | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0 to 1.8s | 0 to 54 | Plate A, held. Hook text from 0.3s. |
| 1.8 to 2.1s | 54 to 63 | Dust enters frame. Screen or add blend mode over Plate A. Plate A also starts a 2 percent scale push at frame 54, which sells the impact. |
| 2.1 to 2.3s | 63 to 69 | **Peak occlusion. Cut to Plate B here**, on the frame where dust covers the most area. The cut must be invisible, so it goes on the densest frame, not before it. |
| 2.3 to 3.4s | 69 to 102 | Dust clears over Plate B. Reduce dust opacity to zero across these 33 frames. Plate B's push is already running. |
| 3.4 to 6.2s | 102 to 186 | Plate B clean, push continuing. Disclaimer if the after is a render. |
| 6.2 to 8.0s | 186 to 240 | Price and MahaRERA lockup fades in. Hold 20 frames clean at the end. |

**The two things people get wrong:**

1. Cutting too early. If you cut before peak occlusion the audience sees both shots and
   the trick collapses. Step through frame by frame and find the densest frame. Cut
   there.
2. Clearing too fast. A dust clear under 20 frames feels like a wipe. 30 to 36 frames is
   the range where it feels physical.

**Audio:** a low whoosh rising into the dust, landing with a soft impact on the cut, then
the music comes up as the dust clears. A silent dust transition feels weightless. Drop
the ambient to zero for 6 frames at the impact and bring it back. That momentary gap is
what makes it hit.

---

## Part E: text on screen

- **0.3s, upper third, 54pt:** the setup half of a two-part line.
  `Was: 1974 chawl` / `Now: 2 BHK, 620 carpet`
  Part one on Plate A, part two appearing at 3.6s on Plate B. The dust separating them
  makes the pair land.
- Disclaimer from the first frame of Plate B if the after is a render.
- Lockup from 6.2s.

## Part G: honest limitation

This format has a short shelf life on any one account. It is a trick, and once your
followers have seen it three times it stops surprising them. The reference reels perform
because viewers were seeing the effect for the first time.

Use it to open a series or to mark a milestone, not as your weekly format. Formats 16
and 19 are what you run on a schedule. This one you spend.
